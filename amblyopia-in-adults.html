Basic Condition Insight

Amblyopia (Lazy Eye) Overview

Lazy eye or amblyopia is a type of vision loss that usually affects mostly one eye, however, it may affect both eyes as well. This occurs when images received by the brain from the eye are not proper due to improper use of spectacles or any other barrier in the image formation in the eye. According to studies, lazy eye is estimated to affect 5% of all adults. If left untreated, it can result in permanent vision loss.

Adult Vision Causes

What causes lazy eye in adults?

Lazy eye mostly occurs in people who suffer from a high degree of refractive errors, such as nearsightedness, farsightedness, or astigmatism. The most common amblyopia-causing conditions in adults include:

Anisometropia

In this condition, each eye has different levels of visual acuity. It occurs when the refractive difference between the two eyes leads to a blurred image on the retina.

Strabismus

It refers to the poor alignment of the eye. In this condition, the patient has one eye turned upward or downward, or both eyes are crossed.

Deprivation

It is the most severe form of amblyopia that occurs due to an obstruction of the visual axis.

Common Eye Symptoms

What are the symptoms of lazy eye in adults?

The symptoms of lazy eye are as follows:

  • Not able to see far objects
  • Wandering eyes
  • Squinting or shutting one eye
  • Coordination problems in the eyes
  • A tendency to tilt your head
  • Poor depth perception
  • Loss of peripheral vision
  • Abnormal results in eye screening tests

Hope for Adults

Can amblyopia be corrected in adults?

When a child suffers from amblyopia, they are provided with eye patching to help the lazy eye improve vision and pick up speed. However, it was always believed that adult patients with amblyopia can’t retain their binocular vision because patching doesn’t effectively work after a certain age.

So yes, against the conventional belief, we can say that amblyopia can certainly be corrected in adults.
